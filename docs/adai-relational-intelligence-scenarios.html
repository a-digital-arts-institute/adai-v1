Scenario Two — The Gatherer Yarns

A practitioner describes
their work. The system
learns new language.

Ade in Lagos describes biodata sonification. The conversation is non-linear — sensors lead to ritual, ritual leads to community. The Gatherer follows. When Ade says something the graph has no concept for, the system flags it as a frontier. When Ade contradicts the graph's classification, both readings stay.

adai-basel.fly.dev/yarn — practitioner:ade okonkwo
Ade
I work with biodata — heart rate, galvanic skin response, soil moisture. But the work isn't about the data. The data is the medium, like paint. What I'm making is — it's hard to explain — it's more like a living data ceremony.
Gatherer
"Living data ceremony" — the graph doesn't have a concept for that yet. The closest nodes are concept:data sonification and concept:bioart, but neither captures the ritual dimension you're describing. I'm keeping your term as a frontier signal. Can you say more about what makes it a ceremony rather than a performance?
Ade
A performance has an audience. A ceremony has participants. Everyone in the space is generating data — their heartbeats, their breathing — and the sound changes in response. They're inside it. And it's connected to place — the soil data comes from the specific ground we're standing on.
Gatherer
That's three distinct threads: participation vs spectatorship, embodied data, and place-specificity. The graph currently classifies your practice as BELONGS_TO "generative art" based on your fxhash work. Does that feel right?
Ade
No. I'm not generative art. I'm systems practice. The fxhash pieces are one output of a much larger practice. Most of what I do never touches a blockchain.
Gatherer
Noted. I'm not removing the existing edge — the fxhash work does connect to generative art. But I'm adding PRACTICES "systems practice" from your own account. Both edges stay, with different provenance: the machine classified from platform data, you classified from lived experience. The tension between the two is the intelligence.
