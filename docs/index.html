1 — Sources

83 validated sources across 5 trust tiers

The sources.yaml registry organises every data source by trust. Higher tiers auto-merge with less friction. Lower tiers need more human review. Selection is governed by four principles: plurality over comprehensiveness, layered verification (every entity confirmable from 2+ sources), relationship density (prefer sources that give edges, not just nodes), and anti-monoculture (no single source type exceeds 30% of ingested nodes).

2 — Gather

the Gatherer — intake edge

The Gatherer operates in two modes, one protocol. Not valueless scraping — editorial intake. The core behaviour is identical: Diversify, Connect, respect the source context, flag what can't be classified. What changes is the trust level.

Scouting extracts from public sources. Every run produces a bias report (geographic, language, platform, temporal distributions) and flags frontier signals. Edge production requirement: every node must have at least one typed edge. No orphans. More than 30% RELATED_TO = editorial lens needs sharpening.

Sensing is paired with a practitioner. Non-linear, relational — follows the practitioner's thread, not a survey template. Their vocabulary overrides yours. Self-authored data has no merge boundary (highest epistemic authority on own experience). Tracks tensions and frontier language.
3 — Structure

the graph holds without collapsing

Signals pass through the intake queue and hit the merge boundary. Three outcomes: auto-merge (practitioner self-report on own data), hold for review (new contributor or low confidence), frontier flag (can't classify → 90-day lifecycle). The graph stores both machine and human readings with provenance. Where they converge, confidence rises. Where they diverge — that's the intelligence.

Every edge carries: signal_id (extraction run), confidence (high/medium/low), source_origin (human_primary, human_secondary, ai_assisted, ai_generated), charge (generative/negative/ambivalent/null), created_by (agent or human identifier). This is the ouroboros defence — you can always trace what the system knows and where it came from.
4 — Render

graph data becomes visual physics

The frontend reads typed nodes and typed edges and turns them into a physical simulation. Without typed edges, the frontend has nothing to calculate from — everything collapses into uniform grey.

Visual PropertyWhat the Graph ProvidesWhy It Matters
proximityNodes connected by typed edges (CREATED_BY, EXHIBITED_AT) pull closer than RELATED_TO. The type determines the strength.Correlation is visible as spatial closeness. Different kinds of connection have different pull.
brightnessGravitational mass = edge type diversity, not edge count. 5 types beats 50 RELATED_TO.A node that bridges different kinds of connection has more visual weight — it's doing structural work.
densityWhere practitioners collaborate, exhibit at the same places, and create works embodying the same concepts — genuine density.Dense regions = knowledge has thickened. Uniform grey = the graph can't distinguish anything.
clusteringNode type drives visual grouping. Practitioners, artworks, concepts, institutions = different colours/shapes/behaviours.You can see what kinds of thing the graph holds, not just a cloud of undifferentiated dots.
sparse regionsFew edges, low type diversity, high RELATED_TO ratio. These are the honest gaps.Absences are structural features, not footnotes. Where the graph is thin is where the frontier signals live.
The data contract: the frontend needs { nodes: [{ id, name, type, metadata }], edges: [{ source_id, target_id, edge_type, confidence }] } from /api/graph. It doesn't need to know about the database schema, CRDT mechanics, or merge boundaries. When the graph gets richer, the visuals get richer automatically. The frontend doesn't change. The data does.
5 — Narrate

the Reader — interpretation edge

The Reader operates in two modes. Narrating mode: reads the graph for visitors — foregrounds tensions, frontiers, absences, or provenance chains. Each narration rotates the angle so no two visits sound the same. Weaving mode: reads across intimate and public layers, surfaces vocabulary gaps, convergence/divergence signals, and monoculture alarms for editorial review.

Forces bridge interpretation and visual form. split = two attractors pull the field apart (tensions). void = repulsion zone (absences, blind spots). intensify = cluster tightens (confirmed connections). bridge = attraction between distant clusters (hidden connections). tremor = vibration (low confidence, contested claims). The Reader produces 2-4 forces per narration that combine additively.
6 — Change

the visitor responds, the graph updates

The visitor isn't passive. Questions become signals. Challenges become CONTESTS edges. First-person knowledge enters at the highest trust level. The graph updates. The next visitor sees a different field. This is the feedback loop that makes the system alive — each pass through the cycle is a different graph.

The graph remembers how it changed. Every signal carries provenance. You can trace who made a connection, from what position, with what evidence, at what confidence level. When a visitor challenges an edge, the challenge is itself a signal. Both the original and the challenge exist in the graph with their provenance intact.
Centre of Gravity

artwork — where the intelligence crystallises

Artworks are the densest possible nodes. A single artwork can hold contradictions — it EMBODIES a concept in TENSION_WITH another concept it also EMBODIES. That relational position, visible only through typed edges, is what makes the graph a canon, not a catalogue. Without artworks, you have a directory of practitioners.

Complete Flow

the whole picture

Sources feed the Gatherer. The Gatherer produces typed signals. The merge boundary gates them. The graph structures them. The frontend renders them as physics. The Reader narrates. The visitor responds. New signals re-enter the intake queue. The loop completes. Each cycle is a different field.

The whole point: no single component holds the intelligence. The Gatherer fills the graph but doesn't interpret it. The graph holds readings but doesn't render them. The frontend renders physics but doesn't narrate. The Reader narrates but doesn't change the graph. The visitor changes the graph but doesn't control what enters. It's the completion of the cycle — each pass through, with new signals, new challenges, new frontier language — that produces relational intelligence. The system is the loop.
