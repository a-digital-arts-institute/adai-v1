One reading of how digital art connects

A public graph of the field's practices — built from sources, and made to be argued with.

Read the fields first. The hubs here are not artists or artworks but practices — generative art, net art, glitch, bio art, and the rest. Artists are joined to the kinds of work they make. The spine is what people share, not the order in which they arrived: a 1968 plotter drawing and a 2021 shader can share a hub without one being said to cause the other.

Every connection shows how we know it. Some lines are attested — the artist named the influence in their own words. Some are shared tradition — two practitioners working in one named field. Some are machine-inferred, read from the metadata and kept visibly apart. Nothing is asserted without a source you can follow. No receipt, no claim.

Influence is rare here, and earned. We draw an arrow from one person to another only where one of them said so. Where a connection feels real but can't be proven, we leave it out — and that missing line is part of the argument. We invent no bloodlines.

Absence is mapped, not hidden. What isn't here carries a reason — under-documented, awaiting an artist's own account, or outside the reach of this lens. A gap is an invitation, never a verdict on whether something mattered.

It is one lens, and it says so. The same artists relate differently under other frames — the market's, the museum's, the practitioner's own. Ours is named, sourced, and revisable. Its references lean Western, and the graph admits that rather than concealing it.

We seed the map, never the work — your work stays yours.

Walk it. Find where you belong, and where you don't. Add to it, diverge from it, argue with it. The shape will change as you do.

The graph is the thesis. Every claim carries its receipt; every absence, its reason.

Why by hand. The graph began with what had clean data — crypto-era and market-legible work. The deeper plurality of digital art — net.art, software art, demoscene, cyberfeminism, tactical media, bio art, live coding — resists automated harvesting, and where records do exist they often distort it: nuance flattened, origins written out. So we add it deliberately, by hand, from the literature read critically — and, increasingly, from the practitioners themselves.

What's shared, what stays yours. The map is a commons: the descriptions, the fields and the connections are openly licensed and free to fork. The art itself is never on the map — only the reading of it. The works stay with the artists who made them.

Who draws what. A research institute is well placed to define the fields — the kinds of practice, drawn from published scholarship. It is not the right author for the lived interior: which scene someone belonged to, who shaped whom in private, what a work intended. That knowledge belongs to the people who hold it. So we lay the scaffolding and leave the interior open — the seed is an invitation, not an enclosure.
